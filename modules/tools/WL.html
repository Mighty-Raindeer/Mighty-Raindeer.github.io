Winston-Lutz Analysis Calculator

The Winston-Lutz test is the gold standard for verifying radiation isocenter accuracy. This test involves imaging a small radio-opaque sphere at multiple gantry, collimator, and couch angles to determine the mechanical and radiation isocenter coincidence.

Understanding Winston-Lutz Analysis

Test Principle: A small metallic sphere (typically 3-5 mm) is positioned at the nominal isocenter using room lasers. Images are acquired at various gantry, collimator, and couch angles with a small radiation field.

Key Metrics:

  • 2D Deviation: Radial distance √(lat² + long²) between the radiation field center and the BB in each imaging plane
  • Per-axis Isocenter Size: Diameter of the smallest circle enclosing the displacement points for that subsystem (gantry, collimator, or couch)
  • Radiation Isocenter Diameter: Diameter of the minimum enclosing circle of all displacement points — the headline radiation-isocenter metric

Note: this analysis works in the 2D imager plane. A full 3D isocenter requires combining lateral offsets across gantry angles to reconstruct the BB position in room coordinates.
